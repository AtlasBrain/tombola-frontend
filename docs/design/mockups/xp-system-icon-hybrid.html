The eight tiers

Technique: bold silhouette body (single tier-color fill) + inner radial highlight for depth + minimal anatomical features (eye, key fins, distinguishing marks). Less cartoony than A, more legible than E. Distinguishing features per creature: • Shrimp: curved body + segmented tail fan + long antennae · Crab: round shell + two raised claws + 4 visible legs · Octopus: bulbous head + 8 curling tentacles · Fish: classic side profile + prominent fins · Dolphin: sleek curve + clear beak + curved dorsal · Shark: torpedo body + tall pointed dorsal + asymmetric crescent tail + visible teeth · Whale: rounded body + small eye low + heart-shaped spout + flippers · Hump Back: KNOBBY HEAD with tubercle bumps + EXTRA-LONG pectoral fins (1/3 of body) + heart-shaped double spout — the three visual hallmarks zoologists use to ID a humpback.

Apex effect — without rotation

The Hump Back badge replaces the rotating conic-gradient rim with three layered effects that don't spin: 1. Static conic-gradient rim — gold accented with subtle coral/lavender stops, frozen in place. No transform.
2. Concentric halo rings — two faint gold rings around the badge, slightly outside the rim. Read as a "ceremonial frame".
3. Diagonal shimmer sweep — every ~6s a soft warm-white band passes from lower-left to upper-right across the inner circle (like light catching gold). Eases hard at start + end so the badge sits still most of the time.
4. Breathing scale — the badge gently scales 1.00 → 1.025 → 1.00 over 5s. Imperceptible at a glance, makes the apex feel "alive" without motion. Combined, the apex still reads as the rarest tier — but doesn't compete for attention by spinning.
